THE MODEL

Each P&G Ventures fund spins off 1-2 companies. Each company in the fund solves a specific problem within an identified public sector thesis that the fund represents.

Public systems fail people, then blame them for the outcome. P&G Ventures builds self-funding companies that fix the systems.

OUR FIRST SPIN-OFF COMPANY

AgentBI

Live Demo

AgentBI is our first spin-off company. It is an intelligence layer for agribusinesses that run large field-agent networks — the people who sell seeds and inputs to smallholder farmers. Agents are hired on informal referrals, churn 40%+ a season, and no one can see who's performing. AgentBI screens and ranks them on verified skills and real sales history, gives managers agent-level dashboards, and gives agents a portable profile and access to work.

THE FUNDS

Fund I

IMPROVING AGRICULTURAL PRACTICES
Building solutions that can meaningfully reshape the agricultural landscape in Kenya and sub-Saharan Africa.

Better seeds, fertiliser and agronomic advice already exist across Kenya and sub-Saharan Africa. The distribution layer that should carry them to farmers does not. Farmer records are unreliable, retail networks are fragmented, cost-to-serve is high, and no agribusiness can see which of its field agents sell. Climate-resilient inputs stall in that layer, well short of the farms they were bred for. Grant capital pays for the field evidence that a fix works, and the company built on that evidence sells it to the agribusinesses, lenders and programmes already carrying the cost. AgentBI is the first; a second agriculture-only company is being sourced from the same fund.

Fund II

INNOVATIONS IN HEALTH DELIVERY SYSTEMS
Improving access to essential medicines and health services in south-east Asia and sub-Saharan Africa.

Essential medicines and health services reach the last mile through delivery systems nobody owns. Across south-east Asia and sub-Saharan Africa those systems — supply chains, health workforces, payment rails — are commissioned by donors, built to a reporting cycle, and switched off when the grant ends. Each is procured for one country, priced against no paying customer, and never tested on its unit economics, so the next programme rebuilds it. Fund II builds companies that own that system. They sell to the ministries, distributors and insurers who need the medicines to arrive, so the system outlives the grant that proved it.

THE MODEL — HOW WE BUILD DIFFERENTLY

Grants carry the risk.
Investment powers the
expansion

Each P&G Ventures fund launches around a public sector thesis. Many ideas are generated under it; the promising ones are proto-tested into companies with co-founders, then 1-2 successful proto-companies are seeded and spun out with a founding team. The studio stays a co-founder, so companies share infrastructure, data, investors, and markets — and don't start from zero.

DUAL ARCHITECTURE: GRANT-READY AND INVESTOR-READY

Every company is designed for two funders from day one. Philanthropic capital absorbs the validation years. Private capital enters only past that point, buying proven demand: fee-generating from first deployment, multi-country from the start, exit-legible from the first contract. Grant-fundable today, investor-ready tomorrow.

OWNERSHIP AND WHEN CAPITAL ENTERS

We target roughly 60% ownership at formation and aim to remain the majority owner through Series A. Grant capital pays for experimentation and validation; the studio funds formation and early commercialisation itself and reserves capital for follow-ons, so outside equity only enters once the company has real traction.

Private investors do not have to wait for Series A. They can commit capital at formation, but that capital is deployed after the grant-funded validation and de-risking is done. Investors from outside the fund can come into individual companies later, once those companies have commercial traction.
